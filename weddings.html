Overview

Wonderful Weddings (WW) is a stress-free, stunning, and sustainable wedding service that saves couples time, money, and effort. WW offers trusted and eco-friendly third-party vendors at transparent price points as well as an integrated cash registry for guests to contribute to whatever custom funds the couple would like to add. ​​​​​​​

Problem Statement

​​​​​How might we convince couples that an emerging eco-friendly, budget-conscious, & stress-free wedding service is possible?

User Research and Insights

We interviewed 22 participants about the wedding industry ranging from married couples to wedding vendors to singles, and we gleaned some user insights about different potential features we should include in Wonderful Weddings.

High level insights gleaned:

  1. Too many decisions to make lead to wedding stress, but not enough recommendations can also leave people frustrated.
  2. Couples put importance on trustworthy wedding planners with good reviews and reliable resources.
  3. Couples want to address their budget and apply discounts where they can in order to save money on customizations that are less important to them.

Testing Our Hypothesis

In order to test our hypothesis, we set up three experiments in the real world to see if strangers would be comfortable attending events together and what information they would need prior to meeting in order to feel comfortable. We sent a follow-up exit survey to evaluate their experience.

Test 1: K-Pop Meetup

The first test involved a meetup between people with similar music interests. The pairs interviewed each other after exchanging a conversation, and the exit surveys showed a 100% satisfaction rate! However, interviewees commented that knowing more about the person they are meeting besides similar music taste would be helpful for more conversation starters.

Test 2: Gimme Gimme Disco San Diego

The second test involved two separate groups of friends who planned to meet at a music venue. This time, we asked everyone to fill out an onboarding questionnaire so that everyone has some prior information about the people they are meeting. However, one of the members tested positive for COVID and had to cancel on the meetup.

Test 3: Rolling Loud Music Festival

The third test involved connecting with mutual friends to attend a music festival. Similar to the second experiment, an onboarding questionnaire was sent to introduce the people first. Similar to the first experiment, an exit survey was sent out for completion, but users were not interested in filling out the exit survey.

User Research Insights

After doing 3 real-life experiments, we gleaned a few high level insights:

  1. People preferred meetups with a group (3 or more people).
  2. We need to figure out what to do if someone is a no-show or cancels at the last minute.
  3. Age was a top concern for participants so we want to give people the option to select matches by age range.
  4. We need to figure out a way to ask about alcohol/drug usage and preference, especially in an environment like a music festival for safety reasons.
  5. Some participants prefer not to fill out the follow up survey.

Onboarding Research

Before jumping into wireframing, we researched the onboarding process of apps 222, Dice, Headspace, and Meetup. We learned that many of them start with a questionnaire to curate the user profile rather than starting with a login screen like many prototypes do. Login screens drive the user away from an application as it is not as welcoming to have to give personal information first before testing out a service.

Business Model, Competitive Analysis, and Go-to-Market Strategy

For our business model, we decided to go with a freemium model where the base app is free to use, but paying $4.99/month allows you to get priority matching along with other perks. Our go-to-market strategy is to advertise organically on social media and through emails and to soft launch our product with USC students in our department, Iovine and Young Academy. We also plan to foster partnership and attend events to increase brand awareness.

Reflection

This project was unique because we were constantly encouraged to go outside to do testing in the real world rather than spending more time on the typical storyboards or wireframes in the beginning. Going outside to test theories and talk to users proved to be very useful in understanding the problem on a deeper level before jumping into designing the application.
